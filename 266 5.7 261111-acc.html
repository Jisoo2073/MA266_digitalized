1st-order equation: \( x' = ax + f(t) \)

\( a \): constant

\[ \begin{aligned} \rho(t) &= e^{\int -a\,dt} = e^{-at} \\ x &= \frac{1}{\rho}\left(\int \rho f\,dt + C\right) \\ &= e^{at}\left(\int e^{-at} f\,dt + C\right) \\ x(0) &= \left.\left(\int e^{-at} f\,dt\right)\right|_{t=0} + C = x_0 \\ C &= x_0 - \left.\int e^{-at} f\,dt\right|_{t=0} \\ x &= e^{at}x_0 + e^{at}\left(\int e^{-at} f\,dt - \left.\int e^{-at} f\,dt\right|_{t=0}\right) \\ &= e^{at}x_0 + e^{at}\int_0^t e^{-as} f(s)\,ds \end{aligned} \]

Variation of parameters

\[ x'(t) = Ax + f(t) \]

Recall \( \Phi(t) \), \( x_p(t) = \Phi(t) \int \Phi(t)^{-1} f(t)\,dt \)

Also \( x_p(t) = e^{At} \int e^{-At} f(t)\,dt \)

For initial value problem (with \( x(0) = x_0 \))

\[ x(t) = e^{At}x_0 + e^{At}\int_0^t e^{-As} f(s)\,ds \]

e.g.

\[ \begin{cases} x_1' = 6x_1 - 7x_2 + 10 \\ x_2' = x_1 - 2x_2 - 2e^{-t} \end{cases} \]

Method of undetermined coefficients

In this course.. \( f(t) = \vec{u}(t) e^{rt} \) (\( \vec{u}(t) \): column vector of polynomials)

usually, \( \vec{u}(t) = \vec{u} \) (constant vector) or \( \vec{u}t \).

Set up \( X_p = t^s P_n(t) e^{rt} \)

  • \( n \) is the degree of \( \vec{u}(t) \)
  • \( s \): smallest nonnegative integer such that no term in \( X_p \) duplicates \( X_c \)

eg: \( f(t) = \vec{u} e^{rt} \)

  • If \( r \) is not an eigenvalue of \( A \): set \( X_p = \vec{v} e^{rt} \)
  • If \( r \) is an eigenvalue (multi 1): set \( X_p = (\vec{v}_1 t + \vec{v}_2) e^{rt} \)
  • If \( r \) is an eigenvalue (multi 2): set \( X_p = (\vec{v}_1 t^2 + \vec{v}_2 t + \vec{v}_3) e^{rt} \)

Example: Nonhomogeneous Linear System

eg.

\[ \begin{cases} x_1' = 6x_1 - 7x_2 + 10 \\ x_2' = x_1 - 2x_2 - 2e^{-t} \end{cases} \]\[ X' = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -7 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} X + \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} e^{-t} \]\[ \begin{vmatrix} 6 - \lambda & -7 \\ 1 & -2 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 12 + 7 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 5 = (\lambda - 5)(\lambda + 1) \]\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda &= 5 & \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -7 \\ 1 & -7 \end{pmatrix} && \vec{v}_1 &= \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ \lambda_2 &= -1 & \begin{pmatrix} 7 & -7 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} && \vec{v}_2 &= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned} \]\[ X_c = c_1 \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} e^{5t} + c_2 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} e^{-t} \]

With \( (0, 5, 0) \) and \( (0, -1, 0) \), and form \( \left( P_m(t) e^{rt} \begin{matrix} \sin kt \\ \cos kt \end{matrix} \right) \).

\( f(t) = \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + e^{-t} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \); \( e^{-t} \) duplicates \( \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} e^{-t} \).

For \( \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \), \( t^s \vec{v}_1 \), choose \( s = 0 \implies \vec{v}_1 \).

For \( e^{-t} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \), \( t^s \vec{v}_2 e^{-t} \), choose \( s = 1 \implies (\vec{v}_2 t + \vec{v}_3) e^{-t} \).

Particular Solution \( X_p \)

\[ \begin{aligned} X_p &= \vec{v}_1 + (\vec{v}_2 t + \vec{v}_3) e^{-t} \\ &= \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix} + \left( \begin{bmatrix} c \\ d \end{bmatrix} t + \begin{bmatrix} c' \\ d' \end{bmatrix} \right) e^{-t} \end{aligned} \]

Remark:

  1. If there is no "duplicates", the particular solution\[ X_p = \vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_2 e^{-t} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} c \\ d \end{bmatrix} e^{-t} \]
  2. Check the difference from \( n \)-th order linear equation (there is no \( \begin{bmatrix} c' \\ d' \end{bmatrix} e^{-t} \))
  3. More precisely, if \( c' = d' \), \( \begin{bmatrix} c' \\ d' \end{bmatrix} e^{-t} \) duplicates \( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} e^{-t} \) in \( X_c \).

    If \( c' = d' \), \( \begin{bmatrix} c' \\ d' \end{bmatrix} e^{-t} \) does not duplicate.

\[ X_p = \begin{bmatrix} a + c t e^{-t} + c' e^{-t} \\ b + d t e^{-t} + d' e^{-t} \end{bmatrix} \]\[ X_p' = \begin{bmatrix} c e^{-t} - c t e^{-t} - c' e^{-t} \\ d e^{-t} - d t e^{-t} - d' e^{-t} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (c - c') e^{-t} - c t e^{-t} \\ (d - d') e^{-t} - d t e^{-t} \end{bmatrix} \]

Matrix Differential Equation Solution

The derivation for the particular solution vector \( y_p \) is as follows:

\[ \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -7 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} x_p + \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ -2e^{-t} \end{pmatrix} \]

Substituting the form of the solution:

\[ \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -7 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a + cte^{-t} + c'e^{-t} \\ b + dte^{-t} + d'e^{-t} \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ -2e^{-t} \end{pmatrix} \]

Expanding the matrix multiplication:

\[ = \begin{pmatrix} 6a - 7b + 10 + (6c' - 7d')e^{-t} + (6c - 7d)te^{-t} \\ a - 2b + (c' - 2d' - 2)e^{-t} + (c - 2d)te^{-t} \end{pmatrix} \]

Equating coefficients leads to three systems of equations:

\( \begin{cases} 6a - 7b + 10 = 0 \\ a - 2b = 0 \end{cases} \)
\( \begin{cases} 6c' - 7d' = c - c' \\ c' - 2d' - 2 = d - d' \end{cases} \)
\( \begin{cases} 6c - 7d = -c \\ c - 2d = -d \end{cases} \)

Solving the systems:

  • From the first system: \( a = 2b \), \( 5b + 10 = 0 \implies b = -2, a = -4 \).
  • From the third system: \( 6c - 7d = -c \implies 7c = 7d \implies c = d \).
  • From the second system: \( 7c' - 7d' = c \) and \( c' - d' = d + 2 \). Substituting \( c=d \) gives \( 6c' - 6d' = -2 \). Assigning \( d'=0 \) yields \( c' = -\frac{1}{3} \) and \( c = d = -\frac{7}{3} \).

The final particular solution vector is:

\[ y_p = \begin{pmatrix} -4 - \frac{7}{3}te^{-t} - \frac{1}{3}e^{-t} \\ -2 - \frac{7}{3}te^{-t} \end{pmatrix} \]
